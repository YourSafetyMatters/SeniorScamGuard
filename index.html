Common Scams Targeting Seniors

Scammers use fear, urgency, and trust to take advantage of seniors. Knowing what they look like is the first step to stopping them.

📞 Phone Impersonation Scams

Callers pretend to be from the IRS, Social Security Administration, or Medicare. They claim you owe money or your benefits are at risk — and demand immediate payment.

Red flag: Government agencies never call demanding gift cards or wire transfers.

💻 Tech Support Scams

A pop-up or call says your computer has a virus. "Microsoft" or "Apple" support offers to fix it — but they really want remote access to your computer and bank accounts.

Red flag: Legitimate companies never contact you unsolicited about computer problems.

❤️ Romance Scams

Someone online builds a close relationship over weeks or months, then asks for money — for an emergency, travel costs, or medical bills. They may never meet you in person.

Red flag: Anyone asking for money online who you've never met in person.

👴 Grandparent Scams

A caller pretends to be your grandchild in trouble ("I was in an accident, please don't tell Mom"). They ask for bail money or emergency funds sent immediately.

Red flag: Urgency + secrecy = scam. Always call the grandchild back on a known number.

💊 Medicare & Health Insurance Scams

Scammers pose as Medicare representatives offering "free" equipment or benefits in exchange for your Medicare number — which they use to commit fraud.

Red flag: Never give your Medicare number to anyone who contacts you first.

🏦 Lottery & Prize Scams

"Congratulations, you've won!" But to collect your prize, you must pay taxes or fees upfront. There is no prize — only the money you send them.

Red flag: You can't win a contest you didn't enter. You never pay to collect a real prize.

The Top 5 Scams Targeting Seniors Right Now

By Rahi Kulkarni · May 2026

Scammers specifically target seniors because they are often more trusting, may live alone, and sometimes have more savings than younger people. The good news? Once you know how these scams work, they lose most of their power.

1. Government Impersonation Scams

A caller claims to be from the IRS, Social Security Administration, or Medicare. They say you owe back taxes, your Social Security number has been "suspended," or your benefits are in jeopardy. They demand immediate payment — often in gift cards or wire transfers — threatening arrest or benefit cancellation if you don't comply.

The truth: Government agencies never call demanding immediate payment. They always send official letters first. Hang up immediately.

2. Tech Support Scams

A frightening pop-up message appears on your computer warning of a virus. You call the number shown and a "technician" offers to fix it remotely. Once they have access to your computer, they steal your passwords and financial information — or charge hundreds for fake services.

The truth: Close the browser window or restart your computer. Real companies like Microsoft and Apple do not reach out to you unsolicited.

3. Romance Scams

Someone meets you on a dating site or social media, and over weeks or months they build a warm, caring relationship. Then comes an "emergency" — a medical bill, a business deal gone wrong, travel costs to finally come meet you. The requests for money escalate, and eventually the person disappears.

The truth: Never send money to someone you have not met in person, no matter how close you feel to them online.

4. Grandparent Scams

"Grandma, it's me — I'm in trouble." A caller pretends to be your grandchild, saying they've been in an accident, arrested, or are stranded abroad. They beg you not to tell their parents. A second caller — posing as a lawyer or police officer — instructs you to send cash or gift cards immediately.

The truth: Hang up and call your grandchild directly on a number you already know. The urgency and secrecy are always warning signs.

5. Lottery & Prize Scams

"You've won $50,000! Just pay the processing fee to claim your prize." Once you pay, more fees appear — until you've lost thousands chasing a prize that doesn't exist.

The truth: You cannot win a contest you never entered. Legitimate prizes never require upfront payment.

The Golden Rule: If something feels urgent, suspicious, or too good to be true — stop. Hang up, close the browser, and call a family member or trusted friend before doing anything.
What To Do

What To Do If You Think You've Been Scammed

By Rahi Kulkarni · May 2026

First — please know that being scammed is not your fault. These criminals are professional manipulators who trick thousands of people every year. What matters now is acting quickly to limit the damage.

Step 1: Stop all contact immediately

Do not send any more money, even if the scammer threatens you or promises to return what you've already sent. Block their number and email. Every additional payment makes recovery harder.

Step 2: Call your bank right away

If you sent money by bank transfer, wire, or credit card, call your bank's fraud line immediately. Ask them to reverse or stop the transaction. Time matters — the faster you call, the better your chances of recovery. The number is on the back of your card.

Step 3: Tell someone you trust

Tell a family member, close friend, or neighbor what happened. This is not shameful — it's important. They can help you take next steps and watch for follow-up scam attempts (scammers often re-target victims).

Step 4: Report the scam

Reporting helps protect others and can help authorities catch scammers. Here's where to report:

  • FTC (Federal Trade Commission): reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center: ic3.gov
  • Local police: File a report at your local precinct
  • State Attorney General: Search "[your state] attorney general scam report"

Step 5: Change your passwords

If the scammer had any access to your computer or you gave them any personal information, change the passwords on your email, bank accounts, and any other accounts immediately.

Remember: You are not alone. Thousands of people are scammed every year — including smart, careful people. Reporting and getting help quickly is the most important thing you can do.
Prevention Tips

10 Simple Rules That Will Keep You Safe from Scammers

By Rahi Kulkarni · May 2026

You don't need to be tech-savvy to stay safe. These 10 rules cover nearly every scam out there. Consider printing this list and keeping it by your phone.

  1. Hang up on pressure calls. If anyone pressures you to act right now, it's a scam. Real emergencies allow time to verify.
  2. Never pay with gift cards. No government agency, company, or legitimate person will ever ask for payment in gift cards. Ever.
  3. Don't give out your Social Security number, Medicare number, or bank account number to anyone who contacts you first.
  4. Verify before you trust. If someone claims to be your grandchild, bank, or government agency — hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself.
  5. Never let anyone access your computer remotely unless you called a trusted company first for help.
  6. If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Free money, prizes, or investments with guaranteed returns are always scams.
  7. Slow down. Scammers rely on panic and urgency. Take a breath, write it down, and talk to someone you trust before doing anything.
  8. Don't click links in unexpected emails or texts. Type website addresses directly into your browser instead.
  9. Talk to your family about money decisions. Having one trusted person who knows about large financial moves can prevent disaster.
  10. It's okay to say no and hang up. You are allowed to end any call, close any website, or walk away from any situation that feels wrong. You don't owe anyone an explanation.
Tip: Share this list with a friend or family member. The more people who know these rules, the safer our whole community becomes.
